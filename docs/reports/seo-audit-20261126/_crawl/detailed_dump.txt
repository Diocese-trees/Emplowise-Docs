One read for a co-founder, a board, or a bank. The short version: Emplowise installs and then runs the HR structure a growing company needs, so the founder stops being the HR helpdesk. Nothing here is drafted separately for the document. 02 Market gap The gap Emplowise was built to fill. A Pakistani company with 10 to 100 staff has two established options, and both leave a hole in the middle. Option one Informal HR, run on memory. Cheap in cash, costly in attention. Contracts live in a folder, payroll inputs arrive by WhatsApp, leave is tracked in memory, and the first real test is a resignation or a payroll error. Nobody owns the process because owning HR was never anyone's job. Option two Enterprise HR, built for a larger company. A senior HR hire is a fixed salary plus benefits for a role that is not full time at 30 staff. A consultancy engagement often ends with a report rather than a running process. Emplowise sits between them: a monthly retainer, a named team, written scope, and execution rather than recommendations. The price is published. The method is the same for every client. The audit is free because the first useful conversation should start with the buyer's own gaps, not with a sales deck. 03 Buying situations Four buying situations, not four demographics. Emplowise sells to a moment, not to a job title. Each situation has a different first fix. Situation What the buyer says What Emplowise installs first Growth outran the paperwork "We are hiring faster than we can document." Contracts, onboarding, leave, and payroll inputs on one calendar A process failed once "We had a payroll error and no one could explain it." Records, approvals, and a written payroll rhythm The founder is the helpdesk "Every people question ends up with me." A named HR owner, policies, and manager scripts A document is needed "I need something credible to show my board or my bank." Policies, structure, and a monthly reporting pack A second audience: early career candidates. EmploStart gives career pivoters and first-job candidates the same structured thinking for free: interview practice, ATS feedback, a resume builder, a profile optimiser, and a prompt library. The broader utility adds planning modules for pivots, reimagining a career, and market assessment. No account, no cost. It is a public good and an honest demonstration of how the team thinks about people operations. 04 Scope What is in scope, and what is not. The work starts from the client's yearly goals and works backwards into HR structure, not from a policy template. If a process cannot be explained on one page, it is not finished. In scope Assessment and a written gap map Policies, SOPs, and handbooks Employee records and documentation Payroll inputs and the payroll calendar Leave and attendance Onboarding Performance support and manager coaching Employee relations and conflict support Organisational assessments Monthly HR reporting and ongoing consultation Recruitment execution Specialist consultant input and a dedicated HR representative Out of scope, stated plainly Emplowise does not act as a law firm, does not guarantee a legal or compliance outcome, does not guarantee hiring results, does not take board authority, and does not replace the client's management. Payroll tax and disbursement scope, recruitment fee treatment, legal qualifications, representative allocation, and contract terms are confirmed in writing after the Free HR Audit. Those boundaries are part of the offer, not small print. 05 Method Four stages, one sequence, four documents. Every engagement follows the same sequence, and the output of each stage is a document the client keeps. Stage 01 Assess Audit the current state. Structure, contracts, records, payroll inputs, leave practice, performance habits, and unresolved people issues. A written gap map and a prioritised list of fixes. Stage 02 Identify Separate symptoms from causes. Pin down unclear ownership, missing policy coverage, and process steps that rely on memory. A short action plan with owners and a sequence. Stage 03 Improve Install the fix. Policies, SOPs, payroll calendar, onboarding flow, manager coaching, and the reporting that follows. Running processes, not recommendations. Stage 04 Review Check what held. Monthly reporting, quarterly assessment, and a standing list of open risks. A review pack for leadership every cycle. 06 Services Fourteen services across three pillars. The automation described on the website is packaging and roadmap, not a sold software product.
